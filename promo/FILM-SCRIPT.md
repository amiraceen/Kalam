# Kalam intro film: scene script

A short vertical (9:16) film about one idea: writing out a thought takes a long time, and with Kalam you just say it.

**Look:** high-key and minimal, like a film still with fine grain. A man is shown in silhouette from behind, at a laptop on a black desk, against a soft gradient from sky blue to blush. The empty gradient above him is kept for frosted pop-up cards and captions. The Kalam icon and the coral glow are the only strong colour.

**Sound:** quiet and cinematic, with real typing sounds at the start. The music opens up when Kalam appears.

---

## Scene 1 · The problem: typing is slow

- **About:** typing out a thought takes far too long.
- **Picture:** he types a message with his hands on the keys. The camera slowly dollies in.
- **Post:** a frosted card above the laptop shows his message appearing one letter at a time. It's slow, with a typo he has to go back and fix.
- **Feeling:** a little tedious and restless.

## Scene 2 · The stall

- **About:** his thinking is faster than his fingers.
- **Picture:** he stops and lifts his hands off the keyboard, stuck mid-thought. The camera is locked off with a slight handheld drift.
- **Post:** on the card, he deletes part of what he wrote and starts again, then the cursor just blinks.
- **Feeling:** this is the moment he's had enough.

## Scene 3 · Discovering Kalam

- **About:** introducing Kalam as the way out.
- **Picture:** the laptop screen warms to coral.
- **Post:** the Kalam icon pops into the empty space above the laptop, with a soft coral glow and rings spreading out. It then drops into the screen, and the real Kalam window opens on the laptop.
- **Feeling:** the mood lifts and the music opens up.

## Scene 4 · Hold the key and speak

- **About:** how it works: hold one key (Right Option ⌥) and talk.
- **Picture:** a macro shot of his finger pressing the key, with a shallow depth of field.
- **Post:**
  - The key glows coral and a gentle ring pulses out from it.
  - A small label names the key.
  - The original Kalam recording bar appears, and its bars move with his voice.
  - A frosted card shows what he is saying as he says it: the same message from scene 1, said in a few seconds.

## Scene 5 · Let go, and it's written

- **About:** when he lets go, the text is typed for him instantly, wherever his cursor is.
- **Picture:** he has let go of the keyboard and leans back with his head slightly raised.
- **Post:**
  - His laptop screen fills with the finished message.
  - A frosted card shows the full message landing all at once.
  - A small confirmation pill appears.
- **Contrast:** scene 1 took forever, this takes a second.

## Scene 6 · Say it your way (corrections)

- **About:** you can change your mind mid-sentence ("scratch that", "no wait") and Kalam writes what you meant.
- **Picture:** the same shot as scene 5, with a slow dolly in.
- **Post:** one card shows what he said, with the corrected part highlighted and struck through. Below it, the clean result Kalam typed appears.

## Scene 7 · Private

- **About:** speech is processed on the Mac. Nothing is uploaded, and it works offline.
- **Picture:** an over-the-shoulder close-up of the screen, with his shoulder and headwear out of focus in the foreground.
- **Post:** the real Kalam recording bar sits on his screen while he talks. A small frosted chip with a Wi-Fi-off icon shows it works without the internet.
- **Feeling:** calm and trustworthy.

## Scene 8 · Free

- **About:** no account and no subscription.
- **Picture:** he leans back in the chair with his hands behind his head, relaxed and done. The camera slowly dollies back.
- **Post:** the caption only. The Kalam window rests on his laptop screen.

## Scene 9 · End card

- **About:** the brand sign-off.
- **Picture:** the last shot softens and blurs into the brand gradient.
- **Post:**
  - The Kalam icon pops in with its coral glow, then the Kalam wordmark appears.
  - The English and Arabic taglines appear together as a pair.
  - Hold to the end with grain and a final chord.

---

## Optional ending: different people, one frame

If we want to show that Kalam works in Arabic and English and understands different dialects, we could add a short sequence just before the end card.

- **Idea:** keep the exact frame from the typing shot (same camera, desk, laptop and pose) and freeze-cut between different people at the laptop. For example: a Gulf look, a Levantine look, an English speaker, and back to our Omani lead. Only the person changes.
- **Motion:** one continuous slow push-in across all the cuts, so it reads as a single move. Add a soft shutter sound on each cut.
- **Post:** each person's laptop screen and a small frosted card show a short everyday line in their own dialect or language, with a small tag naming it. Arabic lines are laid out right to left.
- **Notes:**
  - Keep the clothing dark so the silhouette style holds against the gradient.
  - Have a native speaker check every dialect line.
  - Name only the dialects Kalam supports (Gulf, Omani, Modern Standard Arabic, Egyptian, Levantine, and English).
